## The design system

Taken from **samsung.com/us**: SamsungSharpSans-700 headlines over generous whitespace,
SamsungOne body, near-monochrome ink, pill-shaped chips, brand blue used sparingly, and
dark sections reserved for hero moments.

| | Samsung uses | This deck uses (installed on your Mac) |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines | SamsungSharpSans Bold | **AvertaStd-Black** |
| Sub-heads | SamsungSharpSans | **AvertaStd-Bold** |
| Body | SamsungOne | **AvertaStd-Regular** |
| Brand accent | `#1428A0` | `#1428A0` — pill chips only |
| Base | white / black sections | `#FFFFFF` / `#0B0E14` |

`#E0A100` amber is **not** decoration — it means *hidden / imagined geometry* on every
slide, exactly as in the 5-min video. Green/red/grey keep their four-state meanings.

**Light/dark rhythm:** dark on slides 1 (title), 6 (results), 11 (close); white
everywhere else. Same pattern Samsung uses — cinematic dark for the film, white for the
product page. Your video is dark, so the two read as one system rather than a repeat.

**The one repeated motif** is the pill chip: every slide opens with a blue section pill,
and the same shape carries state labels, group headers and callouts.

## Fonts — read this before the venue

Averta Std is installed on **your** machine, not on a random venue laptop. If you present
from someone else's computer, PowerPoint will substitute and the layout will shift.

- Presenting from your own Mac → open the .pptx, everything is correct.
- Presenting from a venue machine → **use `OccluSynth_Final_Round_Samsung.pdf`**
  (included, 12 pages, 200 dpi). It is font-independent and cannot reflow.

Averta has no glyphs for `→ ↑ ↓ ³ ∪ ∧ ○ ▶ ▲ ▼` — they render as invisible gaps. The
generator works around this: arrows and up/down markers are drawn as **shapes**, `x³` uses
a real superscript run, and `∪`/`∧` were reworded to `+`/`and`. If you add text, avoid
those characters or check with the glyph scan below.

## QA

Every change was checked three ways:

```bash
# regenerate (needs python-pptx + Pillow)
python3 make_samsung_deck.py          # prints any text that overflows its box
```

Also run against the output: a renderer that draws the real .pptx geometry to PNG (catches
overlap/overflow), a glyph scan (catches invisible characters), and the OOXML schema
validator. Current status: **no fit warnings, no overflow, every character renders,
all validations passed.**

One thing to know if you edit text: list items whose width lands within ~3% of their box
get an extra line allocated on purpose, so a slightly different line-break in PowerPoint
can never collide two rows. The cost is an occasional wider gap; keep list items either
comfortably short or clearly two lines.
